Neak Pean

Feb-Mar 2026 Historical Site 1 min read Part of: Southeast Asia Grand Tour

Overview

Neak Pean is one of Angkor's most distinctive sites and one of our favorites.

The name means "entwined serpents," referring to naga forms around the central sanctuary. Jayavarman VII built it near the end of the 12th century on an artificial island in the Jayatataka reservoir. APSARA describes a cruciform arrangement of pools symbolizing the miraculous Himalayan lake Anavatapta, whose waters were believed to have healing power.

Neak Pean is sometimes described as a hospital or spa that balanced the body's four elements. It is better understood as a ritual healing landscape than a literal medical facility: the main pool and four smaller basins symbolized purification and cure.

The Experience

The sanctuary itself is small. The setting is the real event. You reach it along a wooden walkway across the reservoir, a beautiful approach that pairs naturally with nearby Preah Khan.

During our visit, leafless trees stood in the water, lotus flowers were open, and a lightly clouded sky reflected across the lake. After the crowded temples, Neak Pean felt peaceful and gave us room to breathe.

For parents: the route was about 80 percent stroller-friendly, making it one of the easiest Angkor stops with a baby.
