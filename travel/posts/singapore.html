Singapore

Singapore

Mar 2026 City Guide 7 min read Part of: Southeast Asia Grand Tour

Overview

We spent four days in Singapore during our 2026 Southeast Asia trip. It was the least traditionally touristy stop on the route. A trip only for sightseeing might become repetitive, but Singapore is an excellent flight hub and possibly one of the most practical places in the region to live, assuming heat and humidity are acceptable.

Changi Airport and Transport

Changi's reputation for speed was deserved. After submitting the SG Arrival Card in advance, we used automated immigration and cleared it in roughly five minutes. The luggage was already waiting, and we were outside two minutes later. On departure there was again no passport queue. Security is handled closer to the gates rather than through one central checkpoint, which is convenient for passengers but means you should not leave gate time to chance.

Tax-free refund was also simple: the system read our passport and returned the money to the card without asking for a stack of receipts.

The famous Jewel waterfall is not inside the secure terminal flow. We hoped to see it but arrived before its attractions opened. The SATS Premier Lounge was weak after Vietnam and Cambodia, especially at breakfast. Harry's in Terminal 1 accepts LoungeKey-style passes and was the better choice for eggs and an English breakfast.

Be careful with exchange rates. With the market near 1.28 SGD per unit of our reference currency, a hotel machine gave us only 1.04. We had selected cash in Grab and could not change the payment method later, so we swallowed the bad rate. Arrange a small amount of cash at the airport in case your ride does not accept a card.

The metro is large, comprehensive, and extremely well signposted. Its exits have enough detail to be a separate transport system.

City and Daily Life

Singapore has used UTC+8 since 1982, which puts it one hour ahead of nearby countries on our route and ahead of its natural solar time. The history is more complicated than the neat "matching China for trade" explanation often repeated.

The number of ships visible from the plane was extraordinary, more than I remember waiting in the Bosphorus.

People felt split between warm and distant. The easygoing Southeast Asian friendliness was less consistent. Living costs are high and people seem to work hard. A 63-year-old Grab driver told us he could afford only one week of holiday before daily expenses forced him back to work. Retirement and finding work after 50 sounded like real anxieties.

That driver rented his car by the day to work for Grab because owning a car is extremely expensive. The model reminded me of renting a taxi plate in Turkey.

Singapore resembles a greener, more comfortable Hong Kong. Queuing is almost a civic religion; restaurants may have separate walk-in and reservation lines. Trains played warnings explaining that harassment is a crime and can lead to imprisonment or caning.

Restaurants and hotels were very baby-friendly and usually offered proper child seats and sets. People were social and often started conversations about our daughter. Holding lifts for strangers was common, something I last noticed so consistently in Japan.

Many migrant workers rested in parks and public spaces. Their presence never created a safety problem. The city felt orderly even when crowded.

In the evening, running crews suddenly appeared everywhere. Hong Kong also has many runners, but Singapore's organized groups were on another level.

Covered walkways connect malls and office towers to protect people from sun and rain, yet the network can feel like a maze. Even in a carefully planned city, odd details remain: a normal staircase before an escalator, or a covered exit open enough at the sides to let rain blow in.

Free tap water was standard in restaurants, and hotel tap water tasted fine. The disappointing part was the lack of public refill fountains in parks. We found only one near the Orchid Garden despite being told they were everywhere.

All four evenings brought rain. That was our experience at the transition out of the northeast monsoon, not proof that rain follows an evening schedule. Carry an umbrella even when the forecast looks harmless.

Dengue warnings were visible, so repellent makes sense in parks. Wild chickens and roosters wandered around, apparently thriving with few predators. Dogs were surprisingly uncommon.

Food-court tray returns sometimes separated halal and non-halal items, a thoughtful detail in a multicultural city.

Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay

Our first day went to Marina Bay Sands and Gardens by the Bay and still felt too short, despite 30,000 steps.

Flower Dome and Cloud Forest cost 46 SGD together during our visit. Flower Dome contains plants from many climates, including large olive trees and Madagascar baobabs. Cloud Forest has a striking indoor waterfall, but the Jurassic World overlay filled the space with dinosaur sounds and wore us down.

Supertree Grove is free. The structures are more than decoration: official material describes environmental functions including solar energy collection on some trees, rainwater management, and exhaust-air roles connected with the conservatories. Read about them before dismissing them as giant Instagram props.

The SkyPark Observation Deck cost 40 SGD and involved roughly a 15-minute wait. The view was excellent, and drinks and snacks were available at the top.

Satay by the Bay is a safe place to sample local and regional food, especially seafood and street-food-style dishes. Quality varies wildly. Check recent reviews before choosing a stall. Marina BBQ Seafood gave us an empty spring roll and earned a very honest one star.

Merlion Park offers a good view back toward Marina Bay Sands. The water-spitting lion is still funny.

Singapore Botanic Gardens

The Botanic Gardens is Singapore's first UNESCO World Heritage Site. Singapore's National Parks Board also describes it as the first and only tropical botanic garden on the World Heritage List.

The rainforest section was beautiful and brutally humid. The National Orchid Garden cost 15 SGD for visitors and less for locals. Secret Ravine and the Sembcorp Cool House were our favorites. Engineered cooling and misting recreate montane climates convincingly; the machinery is mostly hidden, which makes the temperature change feel almost magical.

The VIP orchid area is not visually essential, yet naming hybrids after visiting leaders is clever soft power. The whole complex combines conservation, research, tourism, and revenue exceptionally well. The Park Side cafe had plenty of food and drink. We spent more than half a day and still felt the scale.

Other Sights

Sultan Mosque, Haji Lane, and the surrounding Muslim quarter may be the most colorful and entertaining Muslim neighborhood I have seen. Temporary food stalls filled the streets around the mosque, while bars lined Haji Lane and its parallel streets. It was also cheaper than central Singapore.

Chinatown is colorful and worth one or two hours. Lunch stalls were mostly closed when we arrived, but temples remained open.

At the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple, the fourth floor had a genuinely meditative atmosphere and photography was prohibited. The relic is presented within a huge gold stupa often described by the temple as using 320 kilograms of gold. I did not weigh it myself, obviously.

Sri Mariamman Temple is a Hindu temple inside Chinatown. Entry was free and shoes came off. It was worth a quick visit.

The Asian Civilisations Museum cost 25 SGD for visitors and was free or discounted for residents. It tries to cover so much that I struggled to find a focus. With only three or four days, it is optional.

Fort Canning Park was empty in the evening and very hilly. The Spice Garden was original; the famous Tree Tunnel was just an Instagram queue disguised as a sight.

Clarke Quay felt mostly like a mall with restaurants.

Food and Hotels

Singapore has almost every cuisine, so research matters more than availability.

PARKROYAL COLLECTION Pickering had small but high-quality rooms, open corridors that made us wonder whether we might fall, a thoughtful baby-shampoo set, an excellent breakfast, and a good view. The first minibar round was included. Even the hair dryer was a Dyson.

BBQ Box Prime turned out to be Chinese rather than Japanese barbecue. The cumin lamb was excellent, the salad very hot, and prices reasonable for the area.

DumPrince served good chicken, mushroom, and prawn dumplings; mushroom was my favorite.

ATLAS is stunning and usually full. We eventually found a gap without a reservation. The drink and champagne lists were excellent; snacks were mediocre, and the children's gnocchi was the best food on our table.

Lime Restaurant was crowded, so we only had drinks. Miracle Coffee made a good cappuccino. CHAGEE had a huge peach tea that was fine. Estuary had decent beer and a fried, mediocre food menu. Läderach at Marina Bay Sands did not stock the truffles we wanted.

Verdict

Singapore is less exciting as a pure sightseeing destination than its neighbors, but its planning, safety, transport, and quality of life make it worth seeing. Use it as a hub and give it three or four days.
