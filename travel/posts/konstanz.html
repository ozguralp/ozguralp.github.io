Lake Constance: Konstanz

May 2026 City Guide 3 min read 7/10 Part of: Central Europe Road Trip

Overview

We entered Germany from the St. Gallen side of Switzerland and crossed at Konstanz during our May 2026 Central Europe road trip. This was my sixth visit to Germany, but the first time the country had been the main destination immediately after a road border crossing rather than a flight.

Konstanz was larger and livelier than most of the smaller cities on this route. Its historic center was extensive and enjoyable, if slightly confusing in places. The lakefront was easily the most appealing part of the city. A festival-like event appeared to be taking place when we visited, so the streets were especially crowded.

The atmosphere changed quickly after Switzerland. The city felt louder and busier, while industry, construction and denser development became more visible as we continued through southern Germany. That was a personal impression from this route rather than a verdict on the whole country.

The Council of Constance

A local man told us that Konstanz had been named the second most important city in Christianity. There is no such official ranking, but the story was attached to a genuinely important event.

The Council of Constance met from 1414 to 1418 and helped end the Western Schism. In 1417, Martin V was elected here in what the city's council history describes as the only legally recognized papal election north of the Alps. The claim about being Christianity's number two city was invented, but Konstanz's importance in church history was not.

Around the Lake

Konzil looked lovely from the lakeside. We did not sit down there, so I cannot comment on the food or service, but the building, surroundings and view were worth a look.

Verdict

Konstanz combined a substantial historic center with an excellent lakeside setting. The crowds made our visit more chaotic than usual, but the waterfront and the city's unusual history justified the stop.

My Place Reviews

Restaurants

Tolle KnolleAug 2026★★★★★

A restaurant built entirely around potatoes, with almost every dish on the menu featuring them in some form. We had the Kretzer fillet with roasted almonds and the pike-perch saltimbocca. Both were excellent, the portions were generous, and the food arrived surprisingly quickly.
