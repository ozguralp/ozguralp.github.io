Ljubljana

May 2026 City Guide 4 min read 8/10 Part of: Central Europe Road Trip

Overview

Ljubljana was the second stop of our May 2026 Central Europe road trip, after Zagreb. It is a small, compact city, but also one of the best-planned capitals I have visited. The pedestrian streets, riverside, squares, and public seating areas connect so naturally that wandering without looking at a map becomes one of the best ways to see it.

Ljubljana does not need a long sightseeing checklist to make an impression. Its real strength is the way the city works as a whole.

Getting There and Around

We drove from Zagreb. Although Croatia and Slovenia are both in the Schengen Area, Slovenia had temporarily reintroduced checks on its internal border with Croatia at the time of our trip. The officers looked at our passports without scanning them, and with very little traffic the whole stop took around five minutes. Slovenia ended those temporary checks on June 11, 2026.

You need an e-vignette to use Slovenia's motorways. For a normal passenger car, the shortest option is valid for seven days and costs EUR 16. We bought ours at a sales point just after crossing the border, but it can also be purchased online. There is no sticker for the windscreen because the vignette is linked to the vehicle registration number, so make sure the plate is entered correctly.

I also liked the road signs showing nearby historic and tourist sites with small illustrations. They make local landmarks much more visible and keep the drive interesting.

The City and Its People

After Zagreb, Ljubljana's buses and some of its buildings looked a little older. The center, however, felt much better planned. The large pedestrian zone, riverfront, and connected squares turn the city's small size into an advantage.

People appeared more relaxed and cheerful than in Zagreb, and I noticed more families with children. English was widely spoken, and we did not struggle to communicate anywhere. The city also has high-quality drinkable tap water, so carrying a reusable bottle is useful.

There were occasional smells of cannabis, though nothing especially widespread. We also saw some visible social problems, including begging and groups drinking beer in parks. One young man attached himself to us for a long conversation almost as soon as we arrived, which made for a slightly unusual introduction to the city.

Ljubljana clearly likes to spend time outside. Cafes and bars were busy, and the overall mood was relaxed and easygoing. With its compact center and young international crowd, it felt very much like an Erasmus city.

What to See

Odprta Kuhna, or Open Kitchen, is an outdoor food market held on Fridays during the season when the weather allows. We happened to be there on a Friday, and Pogačarjev Square was already crowded and festive during the day.

The neighboring Central Market is a straightforward produce market. If you come from Türkiye or another country with a strong market culture, it may not feel particularly special.

Dragon Bridge is one of Ljubljana's symbols. Built in 1900 and 1901, it was the city's first reinforced concrete structure and is considered a notable example of Art Nouveau architecture. I understand its technical and architectural importance, but purely as a sight I still do not quite understand the level of hype around it.

Ljubljana Castle sits directly above the city. During our visit, its interior was closed for an event involving the mayor, and the funicular was also out of service. We walked up instead. The path was enjoyable, but it would be difficult with a stroller. The views immediately around the castle were not as open as I expected, although the setting and the glimpses of the city were still worth the climb.

There is also a real vineyard on Castle Hill, with 1,050 vines planted in 2016. It is an unusual urban detail, but as a place to visit I found it fairly uninteresting.

The square by the University of Ljubljana is beautiful and worth including in any walking route. Even if you do not enter the Museum of Modern Art, its building and the park around it make that part of the city pleasant to explore.

Tivoli Park is the city's largest park. It is spacious, well maintained, and a good place to slow down after walking through the center.

Food and Drink

Wine is taken seriously here. In some places, it seemed more common than beer, and there are many local grapes to explore. I was not especially impressed by the small selection of Slovenian wines we tried, but we did not taste widely enough to make a broader judgment. The number of unfamiliar local varieties still makes the country worth exploring for anyone interested in wine.

Verdict

Ljubljana impressed me less through individual attractions than through the coherence of the city itself. Streets naturally lead into squares and central avenues, while the riverfront, parks, and busy terraces all become part of the same walk. You can cover the center comfortably in one or two days, but it feels like a city that would remain pleasant for much longer.
