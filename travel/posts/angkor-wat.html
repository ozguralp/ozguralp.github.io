Angkor Wat

Feb 2026 Historical Site 3 min read 8/10 Part of: Southeast Asia Grand Tour

Overview

We finally visited Angkor Wat during our 2026 Southeast Asia trip. Often described as the world's largest religious monument, this almost 900-year-old complex was probably our most fantastical journey since Machu Picchu three years earlier.

Angkor Wat is enormous, but it is only the biggest and most famous part of the wider Angkor landscape. The other temples are equally capable of bending your brain.

We explored most temples alone, but hired a guide at Angkor Wat for 15 USD. Tickets for the archaeological park can be bought online, letting you scan a barcode at entrances instead of joining the purchase queue.

History and Architecture

King Suryavarman II built Angkor Wat in the 12th century as a Hindu temple dedicated to Vishnu. It gradually became a Buddhist site, and the complex still shows layers of both traditions.

The visible monument is primarily sandstone, with laterite also used in the structure. Darkening and biological growth on the stone have several causes; our guide broadly attributed some of it to microorganisms.

A vast rectangular moat surrounds the complex. It is so geometrically precise that even a map makes its human construction obvious.

Our guide explained that access and gateways once reflected rank, with distinct routes for the king, clergy, soldiers, and ordinary people. The upper sanctuary was the most restricted. Whatever the exact historical rules, walking through the same monumental entrances today is a satisfying reversal of old hierarchy.

Bullet marks remain visible in parts of the complex from Cambodia's twentieth-century conflicts. Conservation began under French administration and is now an international effort coordinated through Cambodian authorities and UNESCO, with teams from countries including India, Germany, and Japan.

Visiting

Sunrise tours are famous, but we skipped the brutal wake-up. We entered around 8:30 a.m. and left near 10. You could spend much longer. By the return walk, the sun was high and we were drenched. Shade is limited until you reach the interior galleries.

Children under 12 were not allowed into the upper sanctuary during our visit. The site is also poor for strollers, with constant steps and uneven stone. If you arrive with a baby, leave the stroller with your tuk-tuk and use a carrier.

We saw no monkeys. We were told that troublesome macaques had been removed from the main visitor area, but I could not verify how comprehensive or permanent that was.

Our guide also described solar alignments: around the equinox, sunrise can appear above the central tower from specific western viewpoints, while other alignments have been interpreted in relation to seasonal cycles. Angkor's astronomical and symbolic geometry is fascinating, but I would treat the most precise "calendar" claims as guide interpretation unless supported by specialist research.

Verdict

Angkor Wat was a perfect experience. I do not know whether we will return, but it has the atmosphere of a place everyone should see once in a lifetime.
