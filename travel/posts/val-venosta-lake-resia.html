Alpine Route: Val Venosta and Lake Resia

May 2026 Scenic Route 4 min read 7/10 Part of: Central Europe Road Trip

Overview

The valley running from Merano toward Lake Resia is called Val Venosta, or Vinschgau in German. Vineyards gradually disappear along the road and are replaced by vast orchards of carefully trained dwarf apple trees. I had never seen apple production organized at that scale, and it was one of the clearest signs of the region's agricultural technology.

Orchards in the Rain

It was raining, yet sprinkler systems were still running across the orchards. They were being used for spring frost protection rather than ordinary irrigation. As sprayed water freezes, it releases heat and can protect vulnerable flower tissue. Once the system is running, stopping too early can allow the protective ice layer to cool and cause further damage, a process explained in this Penn State guide to frost protection. What looked absurd from the road therefore had a practical explanation.

Stops Along the Route

The Gardens of Trauttmansdorff Castle were unnecessarily confusing to enter and felt enormous once inside. The official site says the botanical gardens cover 12 hectares with more than 80 garden landscapes. I would describe it as a version of Keukenhof built for many kinds of plants rather than primarily tulips.

The castle itself felt secondary to the gardens. We had expected a more history-focused visit, so the experience did not really satisfy us. Anyone who has already seen Sintra or the Singapore Botanic Gardens may want to adjust expectations. Gardens of Trauttmansdorff Castle: 5/10.

Braugarten Giardino Forst, Casette Natalizie, Schloss Kastelbell, Churburg and Fürstenburg all looked like promising stops from the road, but we did not have time to visit them.

The church tower in Lake Resia, Kirchturm von Altgraun, was an excellent place to break the drive. The artificial lake was created in 1950, flooding the old village of Curon and part of Resia. The Romanesque church tower remains visible in the water, a history documented by South Tyrol's official tourism site.

From Lake Resia we continued through superb mountain scenery into Switzerland. By that point, northern Italy had already moved so close to its neighbors in landscape, infrastructure and German influence that the border transition felt natural.
