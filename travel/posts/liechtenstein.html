Alps: Liechtenstein

May 2026 Country Overview 3 min read 7/10 Part of: Central Europe Road Trip

Overview

Liechtenstein was our next country after Switzerland on our May 2026 Central Europe road trip. We entered and left almost immediately. When we could not find anywhere suitable to stay that night, we looked at the surrounding area and continued into Austria, where we eventually found accommodation.

Despite the short visit, the country made a strong first impression. Liechtenstein is exceptionally green, clean, and orderly. Some of the houses looked as if they had come straight out of an architecture magazine, while the mountain and valley scenery was consistently beautiful.

Entering Liechtenstein and Driving Through

We passed through a tiny border post that seemed wide enough for only one vehicle. It looked as if the whole country could be closed with one quick movement. The old gates were also surprisingly attractive.

This was not a passport border within the Schengen Area. The physical customs infrastructure exists for a different reason: Liechtenstein forms part of the Swiss customs area, while Austria belongs to the EU customs union. The customs treaty with Switzerland places responsibility for customs protection on the Liechtenstein-Austria border with the Swiss customs authorities.

We left toward Feldkirch in Austria. The distance was very short, although we still encountered some traffic on the way out.

Vaduz and the Landscape

Central Vaduz felt underwhelming compared with the scenery around it. There was little in the center that made us want to stay longer. Liechtenstein's real attraction is not its flat urban center, but its mountains, hills, villages, houses, and the views that appear along the road.

What to See and Do

Gutenberg Castle has a beautiful position above Balzers. The inner part was closed when we arrived, so we could not explore it properly. The official tourism site notes that the castle stands on a 70-meter hill and that its outer bailey is accessible throughout the year. Even seeing the castle from outside and looking over the surrounding landscape was worthwhile.

The llama and alpaca farm in Triesenberg offers treks of different lengths throughout the year. The weather was too poor for us to try one, but I would consider it if we returned. The standard tours require at least four participants, and some activities run by appointment, so checking the weather and booking ahead is sensible.

Smoking

We entered one restaurant that felt more like an old coffeehouse and was completely filled with cigarette smoke. Seeing that indoors was genuinely surprising. We also noticed plenty of people smoking outside, which felt out of place in a country that otherwise looked so clean and almost sterile.

This was not necessarily illegal. Liechtenstein has a general ban on smoking in enclosed public spaces, but single-room hospitality businesses can obtain permission to operate as smoking establishments. Larger venues may also have a separate smoking room if it meets the legal requirements. That explains how the restaurant we entered could still exist.

Wine

The biggest surprise was discovering that such a tiny country has a serious wine culture. Liechtenstein's official tourism site lists more than 100 winegrowers, although only four are professional producers. Pinot Noir and Chardonnay are among the main grapes, alongside varieties such as Zweigelt, Pinot Blanc, Riesling, Sauvignon Blanc, and Merlot.

The range extends beyond ordinary red and white wines. One official experience at the Court Winery of the Prince includes a sparkling wine reception, four wines, and an ice wine at the end of the tasting.

Unfortunately, we could not find a convenient place to taste local wine during our short visit. The bottles we saw were also expensive compared with neighboring countries, so we left without buying one. Simply learning that Liechtenstein had its own wine industry was still a good piece of travel trivia.

Verdict

Anyone who visits Liechtenstein only for central Vaduz may leave disappointed. The country's beauty lies around Balzers and Triesenberg, in its houses and mountain scenery rather than its capital center. It works well as a half-day or full-day stop while driving between the surrounding countries.
